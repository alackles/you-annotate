# Chapter I.

The author's account of the harbour town—The market and its customs—A storm at sea—Arrival at the island.

The harbour town where I first drew breath lay in a crook of the coast, sheltered from the westerly gales by a long arm of black rock. Its people were *industrious* beyond any I have since encountered, and they reckoned a man's worth by the soundness of his nets rather than by the weight of his purse.

The market was held each seventh day upon the strand. Here were bartered salt fish, cordage, and the coarse woollen cloth of the hill country; and here too the elders sat beneath an awning of sailcloth to adjudicate such quarrels as had arisen in the week.

We had not been three days from port when the sky grew livid, and the master ordered every sail but one to be struck. All that night the sea ran mountainously; and I, who had never before been out of sight of land, gave myself up for lost.

At length the wind abated, and on the fourth morning we raised the island, a low green line upon the horizon, which the sailors greeted with a shout.

# Chapter II.

The island described—Its inhabitants.

The island was smaller than I had supposed, being perhaps a day's walk from end to end. Its inhabitants received us with a civility that I confess I had not expected, & offered us water and fruit before ever they asked our business.
