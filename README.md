# you-annotate

A shared reading space where a class annotates a text together, live. Everyone sees the same book, and everyone's notes appear on it as they're written.

The first text is Olaudah Equiano's *The Interesting Narrative* (1789), but any text can be loaded.

## What a page looks like

Each chapter is its own page, laid out like a printed book with wide margins:

- **The text** is in the middle, set for long reading.
- **The left margin** holds **footnotes**: the editor's notes that "came with the book." A small gray number in the text marks each one; hover over it (or tap it) to read the note.
- **The right margin** holds **structural links**: a thin green line and a Roman numeral beside a passage, showing that it's connected to something elsewhere, usually an item in the chapter's opening summary. The other end of the link has a faint green underline; click either end to jump to the other.
- **Colored highlights** in the text are readers' notes:
  - **Blue** marks *vocabulary*: a word or phrase someone wanted explained.
  - **Amber** marks *commentary*: reactions, observations, interpretations.

Click a highlight to open its conversation in a side panel.

## For students

1. Open the link your instructor shared.
2. Click **Join to annotate** (top right), type your name and the class passcode.
3. Select any words in the text. A small toolbar appears:
   - **Vocab**: ask about a word or phrase. You can leave the comment blank; the highlight itself is the question.
   - **Comment**: share a reaction or observation.
   - **Link…**: connect two passages. Select the first passage (say, an item in the chapter summary), choose **Link…**, then select the passage it points to. The second passage can be in another chapter.
4. Click any highlight to read and reply to its conversation.

You can edit or delete what you wrote, but only from the same browser you wrote it in. If you switch computers or clear your browser data, your old notes stay up, but you'll need the instructor to change them.

## For anyone else

Anyone with the link can read the text and all its annotations without joining. They just can't add or change anything. That makes a finished, well-annotated text easy to share as a reading resource.

## For the instructor

- Log in at `/admin` with the admin password.
- **Load a text** on the admin page: upload a Markdown or plain-text file and choose its title, web address and passcode. Every line beginning with `# ` starts a new chapter.
- **Add footnotes** while logged in: select text in the reader and choose **Footnote**. Students can't add footnotes.
- **Moderate**: as admin you can edit or delete anyone's annotation, reply or link. Your own highlights and comments look just like the students', with your name on them.
- **Change the passcode** on the admin page at any time. People who already joined stay joined.

A text can't be edited after it's uploaded, because every annotation is pinned to the exact characters it marks.
